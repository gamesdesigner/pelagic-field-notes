# Ray and octopus additions

Five new units append renderer destinations 29–33. Existing saved identifiers,
destinations, account records, and chapter order remain unchanged. Chapter 9 now
has six units; Chapter 6 has seven. Each new unit has seven written questions,
a 500+ word foundation reading and a 2,000+ word advanced reading.

Generated numbered plates are in `public/assets/rays-extra/`. The manifest records
the prompts and preserved originals. WebP conversion keeps the original 1536×1024
dimensions. Plates are illustrations, with explicit limitations and a cited key.

- Southern stingray: dorsal disc, eye, spiracle, tail defense and underside inset.
- Short-tail: thick body and relatively short tail; the edited plate corrects the
  spiracle leader and depicts a serrated defensive spine along the tail. Fine
  proportions remain illustrative rather than a measured anatomical specimen.
- Clearnose: paired translucent snout regions, thorns, two small dorsal fins and
  a separate egg case. No stingray-style venomous barb.
- Ribbontail: oval spotted disc and striped tail. Number 4 repeats in the inset
  for the same defensive structure. Number 3 identifies the upper head region;
  it is not presented as an exact pointer to an internal or individual organ.
- Blue-ringed: H. lunulata body plan and a schematic skin inset; rings reflect
  incident light. The article does not generalize every detail to all species.

Risk wording follows the linked museum and specialist references: short-tail
spines can cause severe or potentially fatal injury, but the sources do not
establish a comparative “most dangerous ray” ranking. Blue-ringed octopus venom
can be fatal; hidden rings never make handling safe. No numerical lethal-dose
claims, species danger league table, or unverified conservation categories were
added. Optical mechanisms cite the primary H. lunulata research.
